# Limitations

1. **Only three seasonal cycles are observed.** Quarterly effects are estimated from just three values per quarter, and the first rolling origin has only two full cycles. Model rankings can change materially with additional history.
2. **The backtest is small.** Four origins produce 10 forecast-target pairs per model. The pairs overlap in time and are not independent evidence of general performance.
3. **External validity is unknown.** There is no live operational test, deployment, monitoring history, or evidence that the series represents a current business process.
4. **Provenance and rights remain unresolved for publication.** Evidence supports course-provided data, but the original external source, license, redistribution rights, target units, and calendar anchor have not been verified. The historical observations and uncleared derivatives are excluded from the intended public export.
5. **Forecast uncertainty is not publicly quantified.** The residual sample is too small—especially by horizon—to validate empirical interval coverage. Point forecasts should not be mistaken for precise outcomes.
6. **Calendar dates are unavailable.** The source is represented as generic sequential quarters rather than inferred dates. Periods 13–16 are scenario indices, not verified calendar quarters.
7. **Model scope is intentionally narrow.** Parsimonious models fit the sample size, but the comparison does not establish universal optimality or rule out methods that could become appropriate with more data.
8. **Error subgroup findings are descriptive.** Horizon, quarter, and demand-level summaries contain too few observations for reliable statistical claims.
9. **The future forecast cannot be validated yet.** It is a demonstration generated after fitting all available observations, not an observed result.

The central conclusion is correspondingly narrow: seasonal mean is the best-performing model within this evaluated demonstration, under the documented rolling-origin design and data constraints.
